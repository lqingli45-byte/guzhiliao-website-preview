Read-Aloud Activity Workshop

A planning tool for adult parents or guardians who read with children aged 4–6. The parent uses the tool; the child does not chat with an AI. This English page introduces the product. The first version of the actual tool is provided in Chinese, including for Chinese-speaking families living overseas.

Prepare less, filter less, and follow up less — then open a plan and start reading together.

This is a prototype demo. The demonstration uses preset stories and simulated responses. Real AI is not officially live. The product is not available for purchase, and checkout is not enabled.

Who it is for

Adult parents or guardians who read with children aged 4–6.

It is for adults who want to sit down with a child and a story, but often feel unsure about how to keep the reading going. The child is not the direct user and does not need to talk to an AI.

It is not designed to help you find a new book, or to let an AI replace you as the person who reads with your child. It helps the adult prepare and accompany the reading.

The usual difficulty

You have a story, but the next step is unclear.

Many parents already have a picture book or a story, yet still do not know how to ask questions, what to do when the child does not answer, or how to set up a simple follow-up activity. Asking a general-purpose AI often produces a long list of generic suggestions that still need to be filtered, rewritten, and turned into something you can use tonight.

This workshop is meant to reduce that preparation, filtering, and follow-up, so you can open a plan and start reading together.

Pain points

What gets in the way of a simple read-aloud

You do not know what to ask.

The story is in your hands, but it is hard to come up with a few good questions that fit a 4–6-year-old and the time you actually have tonight.

The child does not answer.

A question lands, and then there is silence. It is not always clear whether to wait, offer a choice, point to a detail, or simply keep reading.

Generic AI advice still needs filtering.

A general-purpose chatbot can produce many ideas at once. You still have to judge what is usable, rewrite it, and ask again until it becomes a plan you can follow.

Preparing an activity takes too much work.

Even a short extension activity can turn into a hunt for materials, steps, and timing. By then, the evening window has often already closed.

What it is designed to do

A ready-to-use plan, not a long list of tips

The workshop is designed to help a parent prepare a short, adult-led read-aloud plan. In the current prototype demo, these functions are shown with preset stories and simulated responses. They should not be read as a live AI service that is already officially launched.

A plan for tonight

It organizes a plan around the story you will read, the child’s approximate age, and how much time you have.

Questions, plus a fallback

It suggests a few questions to ask, and what you might try if the child does not answer — for example, waiting, offering a choice, or pointing to a detail.

One simple extension

It includes one easy follow-up activity that should not require much preparation or special materials.

Less preparation and follow-up

The aim is to reduce the time you spend preparing, filtering generic advice, and asking a general-purpose AI for more.

This is a product direction being validated. We do not claim that it will improve reading ability or produce a particular educational result.

How it works

Three steps from a story to tonight’s reading

This is the intended use flow. In the prototype demo, each step is illustrated with preset content and simulated responses, not with officially launched real AI.

  1. 1

    Share tonight’s story and your situation

    Tell the workshop what you will read, the child’s approximate age, and how much time you have. You can also note any simple materials on hand. You do not need to provide the child’s real name, school, address, or photos. The tool does not provide or copy unauthorized commercial picture-book text or original illustrations. Please use a story you already have, or one you have the right to use.

  2. 2

    Get a read-aloud plan

    Receive a short plan you can use the same evening: a few questions, ideas for what to do if the child does not answer, and one simple extension activity. In the current prototype demo, this plan is a simulated response, not a live AI generation.

  3. 3

    Read with your child, using the plan

    The plan is for the adult to follow. The reading and companionship still happen between you and the child, not between the child and an AI.

Simulated example

A sample interaction, using a preset story

The example below is a simulated demonstration. It uses a short original preset story and simulated responses. It is not a recording of a live session, and it does not mean real AI is officially live.

Simulated example · preset story · simulated responses

Preset story used in this demo (original sample text, not a commercial picture book): The Little Cloud Visits the Hill. A small cloud drifts over a quiet hill and wonders whether anyone will notice it. A pine tree waves, a rabbit looks up, and a child on the path says hello. The cloud feels brave enough to stay a little longer and make a little shade.

What the parent provides

Child’s age: 5. Time tonight: about 15 minutes. Materials: none beyond the story itself.

Simulated plan the parent would receive

Opening: “Tonight we meet a little cloud. If you were that cloud, would you feel shy or brave?”

During the story, you might ask: “Who noticed the cloud first?” and “Why do you think the cloud wanted to stay?”

If the child does not answer: wait a moment, point to one detail (the waving pine tree or the child on the path), and offer a simple choice, such as “shy or brave?” You can then keep reading. There is no need to press for a long reply.

Extension (about three minutes): look out of a window, or simply imagine a cloud shape together, and wave the way the pine tree did. No special materials are required.

This example is for illustration only. It uses preset story text and a simulated response. It is not a live AI result.
