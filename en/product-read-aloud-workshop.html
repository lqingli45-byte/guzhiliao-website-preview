Read-Aloud Activity Workshop

A planning tool for adult parents or guardians who read with children aged 4–6. The parent uses the tool; the child does not chat with an AI. The current V0.3 simulated prototype already has a Chinese and English interface.

Prepare less, filter less, and follow up less — then open a plan and start reading together.

This is a V0.3 bilingual simulated prototype. The plan, the on-the-spot follow-up, and the language switch described below are already implemented with preset stories and simulated responses. They are not real-time AI. A public demo URL is not connected yet, and the product is not available for purchase.

Who it is for

Adult parents or guardians who read with children aged 4–6.

It is for adults who want to sit down with a child and a story, but often feel unsure about how to keep the reading going. The child is not the direct user and does not need to talk to an AI.

It is not designed to help you find a new book, or to let an AI replace you as the person who reads with your child. It helps the adult prepare and accompany the reading.

The usual difficulty

You have a story, but the next step is unclear.

Many parents already have a picture book or a story, yet still do not know how to ask questions, what to do when the child does not answer, or how to set up a simple follow-up activity. Asking a general-purpose AI often produces a long list of generic suggestions that still need to be filtered, rewritten, and turned into something you can use tonight.

This workshop is meant to reduce that preparation, filtering, and follow-up, so you can open a plan and start reading together.

Pain points

What gets in the way of a simple read-aloud

You do not know what to ask.

The story is in your hands, but it is hard to come up with a few good questions that fit a 4–6-year-old and the time you actually have tonight.

The child does not answer.

A question lands, and then there is silence. It is not always clear whether to wait, offer a choice, point to a detail, or simply keep reading.

Generic AI advice still needs filtering.

A general-purpose chatbot can produce many ideas at once. You still have to judge what is usable, rewrite it, and ask again until it becomes a plan you can follow.

Preparing an activity takes too much work.

Even a short extension activity can turn into a hunt for materials, steps, and timing. By then, the evening window has often already closed.

Simulated features already built, and real AI not yet connected

Already implemented in the simulated prototype

  • A Chinese and English interface. Switching languages keeps the story text, the current step, and this round of on-the-spot feedback.
  • You can load the original sample, How Can We Get the Drawing There? (怎样把画送过去), or enter a story you have the right to use.
  • You can choose about age 4, 5, or 6; about 5, 10, 15, or 20 minutes tonight; and simple materials such as paper and a pen.
  • A preset plan can be opened and followed step by step, with back, next, skip, an optional activity, and an early finish.
  • During a step, you can open “How did your child respond?”, try another approach, take a break, or end the read-aloud, using preset replies.
  • You can try changing a step, switching to a non-drawing activity, adopting or keeping the current version, undoing once, or switching to a 5-minute shorter plan from the current step.

How it works

Three steps from tonight’s story to a plan you can follow

These three steps are already implemented in the V0.3 simulated prototype. The plan and the follow-up replies are preset, not real-time AI.

  1. 1

    Share tonight’s story and your situation

    Tell the workshop what you will read, or load the original sample. Then choose the child’s approximate age, how much time you have, and any simple materials on hand. You do not need to provide the child’s real name, school, address, or photos. The tool does not provide or copy unauthorized commercial picture-book text or original illustrations.

  2. 2

    Get a read-aloud plan Simulated

    The prototype organizes a short plan for the same evening: reading, one simple question, an optional activity, and a closing step. In V0.3 this plan is a simulated response, not a live AI generation.

  3. 3

    Read with your child, using the plan

    The plan is for the adult to follow. The reading still happens between you and the child. If the moment is not going smoothly, you can open “How did your child respond?”, try another preset approach, pause, or end the read-aloud.

Featured function

“How did your child respond?”: if it didn’t work, try another way, pause, or stop

During a question or activity step, the parent can open “How did your child respond?” The sequence below comes from the preset logic in the V0.3 simulated prototype. It shows how the flow works. It is not real-time AI analyzing the child.

  1. 1

    Open “How did your child respond?”

    Choose what happened: “My child didn’t answer,” “That didn’t work,” “They don’t want to continue,” “They asked a new question,” or “Something else.”

  2. 2

    If “That didn’t work”

    After you choose “That didn’t work,” the prototype suggests another approach. In the talk step of the original sample, it first suggests “Try pointing”: “You can point if you want.”

  3. 3

    Say “Still not working”

    If the new approach still does not help, you can choose “Still not working.” The prototype does not keep switching forever. It then suggests “Take a break”: “Let’s pause for a bit. We can do something else.”

  4. 4

    Pause, or end this read-aloud

    When the prototype has reached a pause or a stop, you can choose “End this read-aloud.” If you choose “They don’t want to continue” at the start, it goes to “Stop for today”: “Okay. Let’s stop here today.” You can also finish early at any time with “Finish for today.”

If you choose “Something else,” you can type a short note and select “Show another way to respond.” The prototype recognizes only a few common phrases, such as wanting to stop, changing the subject, or not answering. Leave out names and private details. This round of feedback stays on the current page and is not saved with a plan. All of these replies are preset and simulated, not real-time AI.

A real-life scene and a simulated example

How a 15-minute evening read-aloud can go

The example below uses the original sample in V0.3. It is not a commercial picture book, and it is not a recording of a live AI session.

Simulated example · original sample · preset responses

The situation

In the evening, a parent has about 15 minutes to read with a child who is about 5. Paper and a pen are on hand, but the parent is not sure what to ask, and is more unsure about what to do if the child does not respond.

Original sample used here (not a commercial picture book): How Can We Get the Drawing There? (怎样把画送过去). Little Squirrel draws a picture for Little Badger, who lives at the other end of the path. The wind makes the paper hard to carry, so Little Squirrel changes how to hold it twice, and finally arrives with the drawing still in hand.

The simulated plan

First, read the short story together. Then ask just one question: “What happened after Little Squirrel rolled up the drawing?” If you still want to continue, use paper and a pen to draw two of the ways Little Squirrel tried. Then say a brief closing line, or simply put the materials away.

If “That didn’t work”

The parent opens “How did your child respond?” and chooses “That didn’t work.” The prototype first suggests pointing instead of answering. If the parent then chooses “Still not working,” it suggests taking a break. After that, the read-aloud can end without more questions.

This is an illustration of the V0.3 preset flow, not a live AI result. A story you enter yourself uses general simulated steps and is not understood story by story.
